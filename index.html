⚠ DEMO DATA — Illustrative numbers only. Structure and methodology are real; all figures are synthetic. FOM 3.0 · Demand Correction Targeting · $3.1B portfolio · 2,400 pilot stores
Problem
42% of $3.1B FOM 2.0 interventions were structurally unwinnable — stores where on-hand inventory already exceeded demand signal. Physics prevented any impact on wMAPE.
Solution
Redesigned targeting to fire only where on-hand < demand signal, Day-0 or Day-1 timing, and no declining demand window. Validated via store × CID × day backtesting.
Result
309 bps wMAPE improvement on a $3.1B portfolio — repositioning ~$95M in demand. Intervention win rate lifted from 49% to 71%. No DS rebuild required.

FOM 2.0 vs FOM 3.0 — Targeting Rules Comparison
Every rule change was validated through the backtesting framework before deployment
FOM 2.0 Rule (Legacy) FOM 3.0 Rule (Redesigned)
Fire for any store with demand signal above system forecast, regardless of on-hand position Fire only when on-hand inventory is below demand signal threshold — ensures physical capacity to respond
No day-of-cycle restriction — interventions fired on Day 0 through Day 5+ Restrict to Day-0 and Day-1 only; Day-2 allowed with reduced size; Day-3+ excluded
Flat intervention size regardless of demand trend direction Demand trend gate: exclude stores in a confirmed declining demand window
No store-tier differentiation — SNAP and non-SNAP stores treated equally High-benefit-cycle (SNAP issuance) stores receive priority sequencing and larger eligible window
Win rate 49% — nearly half of all fires produced no measurable wMAPE benefit Win rate 71% — validated across 12-week backtest before production rollout

Six phases from historical data pull to production pilot recommendation
1
Historical Data Pull

2

3
Apply Legacy (FOM 2.0) Rules
Retroactively applied FOM 2.0 targeting logic to identify which interventions would have fired — establishes the 49% win-rate baseline
4
Apply New (FOM 3.0) Rules
Applied redesigned targeting criteria to the same historical data — identified which interventions would have fired under the new logic
5
Compare wMAPE Outcomes
Measured wMAPE delta between FOM 2.0 and FOM 3.0 targeting populations across all 4M+ store × CID × day observations
6

Key Methodology Assumptions & Caveats
  • Backtest uses historical intervention outcomes as a counterfactual proxy — not a true randomized experiment. Selection bias from FOM 2.0 targeting is acknowledged.
  • wMAPE is computed at store × CID × day level; aggregate improvement is a weighted average by demand volume.
  • "Unwinnable" classification requires on-hand inventory to exceed demand signal by at least 2 days of supply at time of intervention fire.
  • Day-of-cycle (0, 1, 2, 3+) is measured from the first day of the demand correction window, not calendar day.
  • High-benefit-cycle stores = stores with SNAP issuance on or within 2 days of the intervention window.
  • The ~$95M demand repositioning estimate assumes the 309 bps improvement applies proportionally across the $3.1B portfolio.
  • All store IDs, CIDs, and outcome figures shown in this dashboard are synthetic for portfolio illustration.
