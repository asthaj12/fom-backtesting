⚠ DEMO DATA — Illustrative numbers only. Structure and methodology are real; all figures are synthetic. Demand Signal Backtesting & Targeting · $3.1B portfolio · 2,400 pilot stores
Decision
How do we correct for systematic intraweek temporal bias in demand signals — targeting only the store × SKU × day combinations where a correction is physically executable?
Evidence
The base ML model operates at weekly grain, smoothing over predictable intraweek demand spikes driven by benefit-issuance cycles. Temporal autocorrelation analysis of residuals confirmed the bias was persistent (not random). 42% of legacy corrections targeted timesteps where on-hand inventory was already sufficient — physics prevented impact.
Recommendation
Restrict corrections to Day-0 (issuance day) only. Apply temporal holdout backtesting to validate targeting rules before production. Result: 309 bps wMAPE improvement on pilot population.
Primary Risk
The benefit-cycle signal is strongest in high-benefit-concentration stores. Corrections applied beyond Day-0 in the cycle introduce noise rather than signal — the system must respect the temporal decay of the issuance effect.

Demand Correction System v1 Rule (Legacy) Demand Signal Backtesting & Targeting Rule (Redesigned)
Fire for any store with demand signal above system forecast, regardless of on-hand position Fire only when on-hand inventory is below demand signal threshold — ensures physical capacity to respond
No day-of-cycle restriction — interventions fired on Day 0 through Day 5+ Restrict to Day-0 and Day-1 only; Day-2 allowed with reduced size; Day-3+ excluded
Flat intervention size regardless of demand trend direction Demand trend gate: exclude stores in a confirmed declining demand window
No store-tier differentiation — benefit and non-benefit stores treated equally High-benefit-cycle (benefit issuance) stores receive priority sequencing and larger eligible window

Key Methodology Assumptions & Caveats
  • Backtest uses historical intervention outcomes as a counterfactual proxy — not a true randomized experiment. Selection bias from the legacy correction system's targeting is acknowledged.
  • wMAPE is computed at store × SKU × day level; aggregate improvement is a weighted average by demand volume.
  • "Unwinnable" classification requires on-hand inventory to exceed demand signal by at least 2 days of supply at time of intervention fire.
  • Day-of-cycle (0, 1, 2, 3+) is measured from the first day of the demand correction window, not calendar day.
  • High-benefit-cycle stores = stores with benefit issuance on or within 2 days of the intervention window.
  • The ~$95M demand repositioning estimate assumes the 309 bps improvement applies proportionally across the $3.1B portfolio.
  • All store IDs, SKUs, and outcome figures shown in this dashboard are synthetic for portfolio illustration.
